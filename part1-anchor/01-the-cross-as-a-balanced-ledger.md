# The cross as a balanced ledger, not amnesty

Amnesty fails at acknowledging the loss. The hurt party swallows what happened,
the wrong gets waved off, and the one who caused it walks away with nothing
changed. Pure retribution does acknowledge the loss, and it makes the offender
pay for it in full. The books balance. But the relationship stays broken, because
a squared ledger and a mended relationship are not the same thing. The third way
acknowledges the loss too, and then the offended pays the offender's debt, so the
offender can be restored to them.

Paul writes in Romans 3:26 that God acted "so that he might be just and the
justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus." Just and the justifier, in one
sentence, about one act. It reads like a contradiction. A judge who waves the
guilty through is not just. A judge who condemns them has not justified them. It
only holds together because the one who is owed and the one who pays are the same
God. The Father sends the Son, the Son goes willingly, the Spirit brings it to
us. That is what lets us call it a gift. If someone outside God had paid, it
would be a rescue arranged by a third party. If we had paid it ourselves, it
would be something we earned. Because God is both the offended and the payer, it
stays a gift to us sinners.

Bonhoeffer called the counterfeit cheap grace. Grace with no cross behind it.
Forgiveness announced so fast that nothing was ever named as wrong. The real
thing cost God the life of His Son, which is why Bonhoeffer called it costly
grace. Free to receive is not the same as free to give. It is handed to us for
nothing. It was not free for the one who handed it over. There will be Christians
who say they believe and expect the matter to end there. Faith in Jesus is right.
But it is not a transaction that leaves the rest of your life untouched. The gift
costs you nothing to receive. It still reorders the life that receives it.

The ledger is why I keep using the word debt. A ledger conserves. What is owed
does not get smaller because it moved to another account. It is still owed, in
full, just by someone else now. That is the part of the image worth keeping.
Where it breaks is if it makes God sound like an accountant who is satisfied once
a number is met. What was broken was a relationship, not a balance. The number
language is a stand-in for something the number cannot hold. This also tells you
what "goes free" means and does not mean. The eternal debt is paid, and a debt
paid in full is not collected again. What it does not do is lift the consequences
you still meet in this life. Saved from eternal death is not the same as saved
from what your own choices set in motion.

Put it together. The loss was real and it was named, not waved off. It was paid
in full, so justice was not set aside. The one who paid was the party that had
been wronged, not the party that did the wrong. And the reason for paying was to
bring the offender back, not only to settle accounts. At the cross, the offended
pays the offender's debt so the offender goes free. That is the claim this
repository is built on. Everything after it is an attempt to see the claim
clearly, including the places where the tools I know how to use cannot reach it.
