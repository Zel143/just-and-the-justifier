# The conserve-versus-reduce test

> Status: DRAFT (RAW). Drafted from the outline and the README, in your voice.
> Calls at the bottom are yours. Revise in place, then shape to STRUCTURED.

Essay 01 ended on a claim. This essay hands you the tool for checking every use
of that claim in the rest of the repository, including mine.

Here is the tool. It is one question. When something is offered as a picture of
grace, ask: does it conserve the cost, or reduce it?

Conserve means the full penalty is still paid. Every unit of it. What changes is
who pays. That is substitution, and it is what the cross does. The offended pays
the offender's debt, and the debt does not shrink on the way across.

Reduce means the penalty got smaller. Softened, discounted, or dropped. The books
no longer balance. Someone is still carrying the difference, they just are not
named anymore. That is cheap grace, and putting math around it does not make it
something else.

Three examples, since the difference is easier to see than to state.

Conserves. One party takes on the entire accumulated cost of another party's
failures. The total on the books is exactly what it was. Notebook C builds this
with about fifty lines of arithmetic. Model A runs up a debt, Model B absorbs all
of it, and the assertions at the end check that the number did not move.

Reduces. A system shifts its decision threshold so it flags fewer people. Fewer
people are held to account, and that feels like mercy. But the harm the system
was responding to did not go anywhere. It landed on someone. Moving a threshold
reassigns cost, it does not carry it.

Reduces. Regularization gets described as the model learning to stop punishing
every small fault in its training data. That sounds like grace. It is not. The
model did not forgive those faults, it stopped being able to see them. A judge
who cannot see the offense is not being merciful.

The test is meant to be usable without me. Read an analogy, ask the one question,
and you can usually tell within a sentence which side it is on.

One honest limit before you go further. Machine learning has no built-in way to
do substitution. Nothing in how a model trains moves a paid cost from one account
to another. Notebook C does it by hand, in a loop I wrote. That is a fact about
the tool, not a discovery about the atonement. Where the metaphor cannot conserve
the cost, the repository says so out loud, and
`part2-metaphor/where-the-analogy-breaks.md` collects those places.

The math in here can show you the shape of substitution. It cannot show you the
substance. Keep the test in one hand and that caveat in the other.
